HOW IT WORKS - Fire
phaethos · after the classic demoscene fire effect (early 1990s)

The whole artwork is one Python file. The screen is a field of heat.

1. The bed of coals
   The bottom rows are refilled every frame with a breathing pattern of
   hot and hotter, and the whole bed slides slowly sideways with the wind.

2. Propagation
   Every cell above takes the average of the three cells below it
   (left, centre, right). That average cools a little on the way up:
   a gentle multiplicative decay, plus a small random "bite" that grows
   with height. The bite is why flames thin out instead of stopping.

3. The wind
   Each step, the sample is pulled sideways by a fraction of a cell,
   sub-cell, so the columns lean instead of marching off screen.
   The gusts sway the plume without drifting it away.

4. The embers
   Particles spawn inside the flames, rise faster than the field,
   wobble with the wind, and die out on the way. They brighten the
   cell they sit in.

5. The smoke
   What is left when the flame dies - warmth between 0.06 and 0.16 -
   is drawn as dim grey letters that keep climbing and fading.

6. From heat to letters
   Hotter cells get denser letters, from . and : up to 8 $ % #, and
   warmer colours, from smoke grey through deep red and orange to
   white-hot. Each frame fades into the next and a soft glow is added.

Things you can try (for yourself)
   decay / bite   how fast heat cools, where the flames stop
   FLARE_T        seconds between flare-ups
   wind()         where the coals slide
