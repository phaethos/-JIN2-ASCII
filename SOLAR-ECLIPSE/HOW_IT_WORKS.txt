HOW IT WORKS - Solar Eclipse
phaethos

The whole artwork is one Python file. Every cell asks "am I sun, moon,
corona, or sky?" and the answer becomes a letter and a colour.

1. The slide of the moon
   The moon's position comes from a table of waypoints, eased smoothly
   between them: in from the left, a dwell over the sun, out to the right.

2. The jagged limb
   The moon's edge is its radius plus a small sum of sines of the angle -
   mountains and valleys. This is what breaks the photosphere into
   Baily's beads at the contacts.

3. The photosphere
   The sun is a disc with a soft edge. Where the moon covers it, the
   cell is simply not drawn. What remains is the crescent.

4. The corona
   During totality, cells beyond the limb get a fast-fading base light
   times streamers: sharp ridges of cos(angle), a finer ripple, and
   polar plumes brighter near the poles. Strong streamers are drawn
   with / | \ - letters along their own ray. The moon occludes all of it.

5. The diamond ring
   Just after second contact and just before third, one bead of sunlight
   survives on the limb: a small gaussian, plus a cross of light -
   a long ray along the bead's direction and two short ones across.

6. The chromosphere and the prominences
   A thin red rim shows only at the edges of totality. Red loops hang
   at three fixed limb angles, flickering, while the sun is dark.

7. The sky
   The stars come out during totality and fade again. The moon itself
   is faintly visible by earthshine, and only then.

Things you can try (for yourself)
   C1, C2         second and third contact
   RM             the moon's radius
   jag()          the shape of the lunar limb
   TAG_COLOR      the colours
