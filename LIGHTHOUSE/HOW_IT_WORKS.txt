HOW IT WORKS - Lighthouse
phaethos

The whole artwork is one Python file. Every cell of the screen asks
"how much light reaches me?" and the answer becomes a letter and a colour.

1. The beam
   One cone of light on a slow turn (one turn every 6 seconds).
   A cell's angle from the lamp is compared with the beam's angle;
   a narrow gaussian makes the hard edge and a wider one makes the skirt.
   The light fades with distance from the lamp.

2. The flash
   When the beam sweeps past the direction of the viewer, the lamp room
   flares and the whole scene lifts for a moment - the way a real light
   seems to flash when it points your way.

3. The sea
   Foam lines move toward the viewer. Each line's spacing and height
   grows with distance from the horizon, so the near water looks bigger.
   Where the beam touches the water, the cells break into glitter:
   a hash of the cell turns into a sparkle rhythm.

4. The reflection
   Under the lamp, a narrow column of broken light falls down the water.

5. The tower
   The tower, gallery deck, lamp room and cap are simple shape tests,
   drawn as a near-silhouette. The lamp room glows warm and the core
   of the lamp is almost white.

6. From light to letters
   Brighter cells get denser letters and warmer colours. The dark tower
   stays barely visible; the stars wash out where the beam passes.
   Each frame fades into the next, and a soft glow is added on top.

Things you can try (for yourself)
   BEAM_T         one turn of the lamp
   FLASH_AT       the beam angle that reads as "pointing at you"
   HZ             where the horizon sits
   TAG_COLOR      the colours
