HOW IT WORKS - Firefly Field
phaethos

The whole artwork is one Python file. Ninety fireflies, one field.

1. The grass
   Blades are thin stripes of a sine field, bent sideways by a slow sway
   that grows with height, dense and dark at the bottom, pale at the tips.
   Mist drifts just above them. Leaves hang in from the top corners.

2. The flies
   Each firefly has an anchor and a wander: a few slow sines per axis.
   Its flash is a sharp attack and a slow decay, once per period.
   Flies inside the grass are dimmed, as if behind the blades.

3. The wave
   Four in ten flies share one rhythm, with their phase offset by their
   position across the field. Together their flashes make a band of light
   that sweeps from one side to the other, then rests and starts again.
   The others free-run, so the field never looks mechanical.

4. From light to letters
   A firefly's own cell is a * or +; its four neighbours keep a faint
   copy of the light, the way a dark-adapted eye smears a spark.
   Each frame fades into the next, and a soft glow is added on top.

Things you can try (for yourself)
   TG             the shared rhythm
   NF             how many flies
   0.85           how steeply the wave's phase crosses the field
